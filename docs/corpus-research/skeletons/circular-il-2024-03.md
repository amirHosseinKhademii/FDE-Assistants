## I. Background and Purpose

This bulletin states the Department's interpretation of existing law regarding
amounts that must be included when a private passenger automobile is declared a
total loss. A bulletin states the Department's position and does not itself
establish a binding norm; where this bulletin and the Insurance Code diverge,
the Code controls.

## II. Applicability and Scope

Applies to first-party physical damage claims on private passenger automobile
policies issued, delivered, issued for delivery, or renewed on or after
2024-07-01, where the vehicle is principally garaged in this State. It does not
reach policies issued in another state.

## III. Department Position

A settlement offer on a declared total loss must reflect the cost of replacing
the vehicle with a comparable one, and must include the transactional amounts a
claimant necessarily incurs to put a replacement vehicle on the road. A policy
provision stating only that the insurer pays actual cash value does not relieve
the insurer of these components, which the Department regards as part of the
cost of replacement rather than as an additional benefit.

| Component | First party | Third party | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vehicle value | Required | Required | § 24-8-119 |
| Sales tax | Required | Not required | § 24-8-119(3) |
| Title transfer fee | Required | Not required | This bulletin |
| Registration fee | Required | Not required | This bulletin |
| Documentary fee | Not required | Not required | — |

Where the claimant does not replace the vehicle, the sales tax component remains
payable. The Department does not regard replacement as a condition precedent.

## IV. Valuation Method

A comparable vehicle is one of the same make, model, model year, body type and
option package, adjusted for mileage and condition. Where fewer than two
comparable vehicles are located within a reasonable geographic area, the file
must document the search and the basis for any extrapolation.

| Requirement | Standard | Documented in |
|---|---|---|
| Comparable count | Two or more | Valuation report |
| Geographic area | Reasonable to the claimant | Valuation report |
| Condition adjustment | Itemised | Valuation report |

## V. Reopening

A first-party total loss claim may be reopened within 33 days of the written
offer where the claimant produces a comparable vehicle at a higher figure. The
written offer must state this right.
