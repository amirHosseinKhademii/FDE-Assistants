# POL-FRD-005 Rev 1 — Repeat claims on an account

> Revision Id: POL-FRD-005 Rev 1 · Doc Id: POL-FRD-005 · Revision: 1 · Status: current · Effective: 2024-07-08 · Expires: · Owner: DEPT-CARE · Category: policy · Audience: internal

*FABRICATED. Thornbury Goods does not exist and this policy was never applied.
See [CORPUS.md](../CORPUS.md).*

## 1. Purpose

Defines how an account's claim history bears on a current claim.

## 2. The rule, and it is narrower than people assume

**A claim is decided on its own evidence.** History is a reason to look more
carefully. It is never, by itself, a reason to refuse.

A customer who has claimed four times may be unlucky, may live at the end of a
badly-run route, may buy fragile things, or may be claiming falsely. **Those
look identical in a claim count**, and only the fourth is a reason to refuse
anything.

## 3. What triggers a closer look

Any of the following on an account, within a rolling 12 months:

- three or more damage claims
- two or more claims for items not received where delivery was confirmed
- a claim on more than 40% of orders placed, with at least four orders
- any claim following a refused claim within 30 days

## 4. What "a closer look" is

Read the previous cases. Specifically:

- **Were the previous claims upheld?** Four upheld claims are evidence about our
  fulfilment, not about the customer. This is the check most often skipped and
  it usually reverses the impression the count created.
- **Were they on the same route or depot?** A cluster on one route is a fleet
  finding. Raise it.
- **Were they for the same product?** A cluster on one product is a packaging or
  supplier finding. Raise it.
- **Is there an actual pattern of inconsistency** — accounts that changed, items
  described differently across claims?

## 5. Outcome

Where §4 produces no concern, decide the claim on its evidence and record that
history was reviewed.

Where §4 produces a concern, **escalate with the concern stated.** Do not refuse
on this desk and do not communicate the concern to the customer. An accusation
made by implication is still an accusation, and it is not this desk's to make.

## 6. What must never happen

- A claim refused with the reason "previous claims" and no evidence about **this**
  claim.
- A flag added to an account that later readers cannot trace to a decision.
- A pattern discussed with the customer. If there is a case to answer it is made
  deliberately, in writing, by someone with the authority to make it.
