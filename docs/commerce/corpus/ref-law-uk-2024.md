## 1. Why this document exists

Thornbury's policies are promises we chose to make. Underneath them is a floor
we did not choose and cannot lower.

**Where a policy gives a customer less than the floor, the floor applies and the
policy is wrong.** A policy may always give more.

## 2. The short-term right to reject — 30 days

Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015, goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit
for purpose and as described. Where they are not, the customer has a **short-term
right to reject for 30 days from delivery** and to a **full refund**.

Two things follow that get misapplied here:

- **This is not the same 30 days as POL-RET-001 §2.** Ours is a change-of-mind
  window we granted. This one is a statutory right that applies to faulty goods.
  A coincidence of number, not of meaning.
- **An item damaged on arrival is not of satisfactory quality.** A damaged-on-
  arrival claim inside 30 days engages this right directly, and it does not
  depend on our policy at all.

## 3. After 30 days

Between 30 days and six months, the customer is entitled to a repair or a
replacement. If that fails or is not possible, a refund — which may be reduced
to reflect use.

**In the first six months the burden is on us.** Goods that fail are presumed to
have been faulty at delivery unless we can show otherwise. After six months the
burden moves to the customer.

## 4. Distance selling — the 14-day cancellation right

Under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, a customer buying at distance may
cancel within **14 days of receiving the goods**, for any reason, and has a
further 14 days to return them.

- This is separate from, and additional to, anything in POL-RET-001.
- **It does not require the goods to be unused or sealed.** A customer may handle
  goods as they would in a shop. NOTE-ELEC-2022 got this wrong, which is why it
  is retired.
- We may reduce the refund for handling beyond that, and must be able to justify
  the reduction.

## 5. Delivery

Goods must be delivered without undue delay and within 30 days unless agreed
otherwise. Where delivery fails and a time was essential, or a second agreed time
is missed, the customer may treat the contract as at an end and get their money
back.

**The risk in the goods sits with us until the customer has physical possession.**
A parcel damaged in transit is our problem and not the customer's, whatever any
carrier contract says between us and a carrier.

## 6. What cannot be excluded

The rights above cannot be excluded or restricted by a term, a policy, a
returns page, or anything a customer clicked. A term attempting it is not
binding on them.

## 7. How to use this document

Cite it where a policy and the floor diverge. It is the reason a claim outside
our window is **weighed rather than dismissed** (POL-DOA-002 §3), and the reason
a conflict between a customer's expectation and our configuration is an
escalation rather than a refusal.

**If an answer refuses a customer something this document appears to grant, that
answer needs a human before it is given.**
